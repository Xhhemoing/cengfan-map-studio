### 3. R5-1 cap vs the 8 MiB API accept limit — real residue, NOT a blocker
- The band exists on two levels. Per-room: the transaction body limit (`min(maxJsonBodyBytes, DEFAULT_MAX_ROOM_TRANSACTION_BYTES)` = 8 MiB) bounds the *request*, but the persisted record adds access records, up to 256 operation-history entries, and room metadata on top of the package — a package the API accepts near the limit yields a record over `MAX_PERSISTED_ROOM_BYTES` and is skipped; with dense op history the overhead can be far more than envelope-scale. Snapshot level: a record of exactly 8 MiB passes the per-room check and is then evicted by the total budget because the `{"version":1,"rooms":[…]}` envelope adds ~24 bytes. And structurally: aggregate budget = per-room cap means two heavy rooms can never both persist.
- Why this is not a blocker: the band narrowed from 3 MiB (R4) to envelope-scale for fresh rooms; it is **no longer silent** — the flush warn names the room ids and both caps, the envelope records `skippedRoomIds`, and R5-2 surfaces the loss at the next boot; affected rooms stay fully live until restart; and the calibration is bench-honest (12 MiB measured 86 ms — raising the cap to "accept limit + margin" as R4 prescribed would have blown the occupancy budget on this machine, and the implementer documented choosing exact parity instead). The correct elimination is not a bigger constant but **history-first degradation**: `getOperations` already answers a trimmed interval with `VERSION_CONFLICT`/"请重新获取完整快照" (collaboration.ts:721–724), so persisting an oversized room *without its operation history* is safe by construction. That is R6-1, the flagship Round-6 task, together with client/ops visibility (R6-2/R6-6).
