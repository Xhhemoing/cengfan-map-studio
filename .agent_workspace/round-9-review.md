## Answers to the five questions

### 1. Does R9-1→R9-2→R9-3 close the actively-editing member's persist-failure surface end to end? Any dark remainder?

**Yes — closed end to end, every joint pinned, and proven over a kernel errno.** The chain: store records the failure (R7-1) → both ack shapes carry `roomPersistenceFields` with exact-shape pins and a real-flush-cycle clear (R9-2) → R9-4 proves `lastFailureAt` rides the ack and the snapshot GET over real HTTP with a real EISDIR → client normalizes acks through `parseRoomSnapshot` with the same three-literal/absence/garbage discipline as the other three paths (R9-3) → `commitAcknowledgement` projects only present keys (no invented `undefined`, which would read as "server said none") and notes them ahead of the baseline early-return → hook merges through the single `notePersistence` rulebook → App wiring needed zero edits (controller is the whole hook result — verified at `editor-collaboration-wiring.ts:135`) → ProjectMenu prop landed in R8-3b. **Dark remainder: yes, the idle SSE member — bounded and inherent to the freeze.** A member with a healthy SSE stream and no local edits makes **zero** HTTP requests in steady state (heartbeats are server→client on the stream; ops arrive as SSE events; `fetchRoomOperations` runs only on heal/conflict), so a mid-session streak — and equally its healing — reaches them only on heal, version-conflict refetch, or their own next edit. The exposure is narrow (an idle member's own last edits are undurable only if the streak began before the first flush after them) and the stale direction after healing is conservative (a lingering warning, not a lingering all-clear). This cannot be fixed over HTTP for a genuinely idle client; it is an SSE-unfreeze or product decision, and it does **not** meet the unfreeze bar this round. Documented, not tasked.
